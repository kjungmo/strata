\section{Synthetic Experiments}\label{sec:exp}

The synthetic suite uses hand-authored hit and miss patterns, so that the truth of every cell is known. It runs in two arms: the \emph{legacy} arm, the arithmetic of the first release with the explicit parameters of the first release, whose results are those of the first version of this study; and the \emph{sprt} arm, the sequential test at the shipped defaults on the same scenes and seeds (Section~\ref{subsec:sprtsynth}). In the text and tables of the legacy arm, \emph{shipped} means the defaults of the first release (Table~\ref{tab:params}, group ``Legacy evidence model''); in the sprt arm it means the defaults of version 0.2. We ask six questions. Do the two backends behave as one engine wearing two geometries (E1, E4)? Does the shared classifier detect periodic structure, and how does it fail (E2)? Does the live, pruned and repeatedly tested detector control false alarms over a whole run (E5)? Which cells does the graduation rule call Static when they are neither walls nor periodic (E5, E6)? Which mechanism actually stabilises the static layer (E3)? Does the sequential test on the real engine follow Wald's operating characteristic, and how much does autocorrelation of the windows inflate its error (E7)?

\subsection{Experimental Setup}\label{subsec:setup}

\noindent\textbf{Harness and protocol.} All experiments are driven by a standalone harness that compiles directly against the \code{strata\_core} sources, with no ROS~2, ament or colcon in the loop; it lives in \nolinkurl{paper/experiments/}. One command, \nolinkurl{bash paper/experiments/run_all.sh}, builds the core and its unit tests, checks the \code{ctest} suite (one target aggregating the 114 gtest cases, among them ten regression tests for the amplitude defect of Remark~\ref{rem:leak}, nine for the significance test of Proposition~\ref{prop:chernoff} and eight for the spent level of Proposition~\ref{prop:spend}), builds and runs the calibration E0 and the experiments E1--E7, and writes the CSVs in \nolinkurl{paper/experiments/results/}; every table and plot in this paper is regenerated from those files (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}). E0--E3, E5, E6 and E7 reproduce their committed files byte for byte with GCC and libstdc++, and under another standard library the E1 files, the E0 door files, the E6 confusion files and the E4 cell counts of the sprt arm change (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}); E4's timings are wall-clock and are recorded separately. Every stochastic experiment E1--E4 derives its \code{std::mt19937} generator from the constant seed 12345 plus a per-run offset recorded in code (offsets 0--1500), and the seed is logged in each CSV. The calibration E0 uses seeds from 20260928 upward, E5 draws its calibration seeds from 30260928 and its evaluation seeds from 40260928 upward, so no calibration stream coincides with an evaluation stream; E6 reuses the scene seeds of E5's confusion and draws its sweep from 50260928 upward, and the unit tests use a further disjoint range. E1--E4 magnitudes are single-seed point estimates: we report no cross-seed dispersion for them, and gaps such as 0.9253 against 0.9758 illustrate a mechanism rather than a distributional estimate. E5 and E6 are multi-seed and report Wilson 95\% intervals.

\noindent\textbf{What is compared.} There is no external baseline in the synthetic suite. The lifelong-SLAM systems of \S\ref{sec:related} do not expose a persistence core that this harness could drive. The FreMEn per-cell model \citep{krajnik2017fremen,krajnik2016persistent,fremenrepo}, with its amplitude-ranked component selection, could be driven by the same streams, but we have not run it, so every comparison below is internal; the evaluation on real data (Section~\ref{sec:realeval}) compares four other evidence rules. The centred amplitude-only rule kept in Table~\ref{tab:fix} thresholds FreMEn-style coefficients at one fixed period; it is \sname's own earlier rule, not an implementation of FreMEn. Instead E1 and E4 compare the two \sname\ backends, each driven in its native geometry by its own seeded stream (\code{grid2d} on the integer lattice, \code{voxel3d} continuously); because both call the identical engine, any divergence arises in $\kappa_b$, $\rho_b$ or their input sampling. E2 and E3 drive \code{LayeredMap} directly with no backend at all, which by \eqref{eq:objective} characterises the classifier for whichever backend supplies the ids. Each harness sets its own parameters in code (Table~\ref{tab:eparams} for E1--E4; E0, E5 and E6 use the E2 parameters or the shipped defaults, as stated with each); in particular every experiment, E0 and E5 included, runs with one window per tick ($L{=}1$), so \emph{window} and \emph{frame} coincide.

\noindent\textbf{Metrics.} Static-set precision, recall and F1 against a known wall set (E1, E3); per-read-out false-alarm rates on pure-noise cells and the read-out length from which a door stays Periodic (E0); the trajectory-level false-alarm rate, i.e.\ the share of non-periodic cells labelled Periodic at \emph{some} window up to a horizon $N$, the per-window labelling rate, and four-class precision and recall (E5); Periodic-class true- and false-positive rates against ground truth and the dominant amplitude $a$ (E2); \emph{flicker}, the number of per-window toggles of a wall cell's Static flag (E3); the share of Bernoulli($m$) cells labelled Static (E6); final live-cell count, estimated memory at 56~B per cell and, where it was recorded reliably, latency per \code{integrate()} call (E4).

\noindent\textbf{Scenarios and hardware.} E0 runs first and fixes the single-read-out level (\S\ref{subsec:fremen}): iid Bernoulli($m$) streams at $T/H\in\{8/3, 24/2\}$, $m\in\{0.05,0.1,0.2,\dots,0.9,0.95\}$ and 12 read-out lengths $n\in\{8,\dots,100\}$ for $T{=}8$ and 8 lengths $n\in\{24,\dots,240\}$ for $T{=}24$ (listed in \code{e0\_calibration.cpp}), with 20{,}000 streams per point; 3000 Bernoulli($m$) clutter cells ($m\in\{0.3,0.5,0.7\}$) through the live \code{LayeredMap} with the E2 parameters, read out at every $n=8$--$100$; and four doors (4-on/4-off, 2-on/2-off, 3-on/5-off, and 4-on/4-off with 10\% flipped windows) in 40 runs each at random phase offsets. E1: a 161-cell static cross (the segments $y{=}60$ and $x{=}60$ over $[20,100]$) hit every window, plus 5, 25 or 100 fresh random movers per window, for 40 windows, on a $120{\times}120$ grid and a voxel map, both at resolution 1.0, once with periodicity off and once with it on at the shipped $T{=}24$, $H{=}2$. E2: three periodic doors (period 8 at 4-on/4-off and 2-on/6-off, period 4 at 2-on/2-off), a constant wall and four Bernoulli(0.5) aperiodic controls, with $T{=}8$, $H{=}3$, read out at 64 windows and, in a sweep, after every length $n=8$--$100$ (a fresh map per length). E3: 50 wall cells observed occupied with probability 0.6 per window among 10 movers per window, 80 windows, the same noise replayed for all 36 configurations of $\pgrad\in\{0.6,0.7,0.8,0.9\}\times\pdem\in\{0.3,0.5,\pgrad\}\times\lambda\in\{0.90,0.97,1.0\}$, with periodicity off and again with it on at the shipped defaults. E5: Bernoulli($m$) clutter, $m=0.1,\dots,0.9$, plus always-occupied, always-free and temporally correlated cells (a two-state Markov chain with mean 0.5 and mean dwell 10 windows), 2000 cells per configuration (10 seeds of 200), through the live \code{LayeredMap} with the E2 and with the shipped parameters, touched every window or independently with probability $q{=}0.5$, over 1024 windows, labelled after every window; four door types for detection delay; and 20 E2-style scenes for the confusion of Table~\ref{tab:e5conf}. The calibration half of E5 fixes the shipped level on its own seeds before the evaluation half runs. E6, added after E5 showed many false Static labels, repeats that confusion on the same 20 scene seeds under the E2 parameters with $\lambda{=}0.97$ and under the shipped defaults (whose doors have the same duty cycles at three times the period, since $T{=}24$), and sweeps Bernoulli($m$) cells over $m=0.05,0.10,\dots,0.95$, 2000 cells per point (10 seeds of 200), touched every window for 256 windows, under four parameter sets: E2, E2 with $\lambda{=}0.97$, the shipped defaults, and the shipped defaults with $\lmiss=-\lhit$. All E1--E4 results are from the current engine (centred coefficients and the test at $\delta{=}0.2$ spent over the touch count). The same harness run against three earlier engines is kept for comparison (Table~\ref{tab:fix}): v0.1.0, the centred engine with the amplitude-only rule $a\ge\amin$, and the test at the single-read-out level $\delta{=}0.1$. E4: 150 random hits per window for 20 windows at nominal extents $100^2$, $250^2$ and $500^2$, resolution 1.0, periodicity off, each configuration run 15 times on a fresh map after one discarded warm-up run. E4 alone reads the wall clock. \EfourSetup{} Its live-cell counts follow only from ray geometry and are deterministic.

\subsection{Main Results}\label{subsec:main}

\noindent\textbf{Summary of the legacy arm.} Under the v0.1 evidence rule both backends graduate a 161-cell wall by the third window and keep recall at 1.0 through window 39 (with the sequential test of the current engine the same wall graduates at its derived fourth window and no false Static cell appears in any of the six runs); their only quality gap, a final static precision of 0.9253 (\code{grid2d}) against 0.9758 (\code{voxel3d}) under 100 movers per window, arises in the backends' native sampling rather than in the shared classifier. We report and correct two defects of the first release's periodicity test, with before-and-after results. An uncentred Fourier amplitude could demote constant walls. An uncalibrated amplitude threshold labelled Bernoulli clutter Periodic at 0 to 4 of 5 cells depending on read-out length, a mean false-positive rate of 0.331 even after centring. Because the map re-tests every window, a constant significance level still let up to 953 of 2000 clutter cells be labelled Periodic at some window of a 1024-window run; with the level spent over the touch count, at most 2 of 2000 are, on held-out seeds, and the E2 false-positive rate is 0 at every read-out length from 8 to 100 windows. Both 50\%-duty doors are still detected, for a true-positive rate of 2/3, but only from 25 windows instead of 8, and the one miss is a provable interaction between pruning and the amplitude gate. Over 20 held-out scenes the dominant labelling error is a false Static label on aperiodic cells, not a false Periodic one: with the v0.1 defaults 1960 of 2000 Bernoulli(0.5) cells end Static, and the graduation rule cannot separate such clutter from a noisy wall of similar occupancy (Remark~\ref{rem:drift}; Section~\ref{subsec:seqv01} gives the reason). Removing the hysteresis band raises flicker from 54 to 574 toggles at the same decay, and the estimated footprint is a flat 56~B per cell with periodicity off; \EfourIntro{} 4.4--10.0$\times$ more live 3D cells. On this synthetic suite, the results are consistent with a lifelong-mapping core that is written once and shared across dimensions, with the two backends differing in how many cells their geometry creates. Whether the calibrated test improves on FreMEn's own component selection remains to be evaluated (\S\ref{sec:disc}); how the evidence rule behaves on real LiDAR data is the subject of Sections~\ref{sec:calib} and~\ref{sec:realeval}.

\noindent\textbf{Periodicity detection (E2).} Both 50\%-duty doors are classified Periodic, with reference amplitudes 0.653 and 0.707 and Bonferroni-adjusted bounds below $10^{-3}$ (Table~\ref{tab:e2}). The constant wall is Static with amplitude exactly 0 at every length. No aperiodic control is Periodic, so at the $n{=}64$ read-out the Periodic class reaches a true-positive rate of 2/3 and a false-positive rate of 0/5; the two earliest engines scored 1/5 (v0.1.0) and 2/5 (centred only) at this length. Figure~\ref{fig:e2fpr} and Table~\ref{tab:fix} show that this is not a favourable read-out. Over all lengths $n=8$--$100$ the pipeline has no false positive at any length, as with the single-read-out level, against a mean false-positive rate of 0.363 (v0.1.0) and 0.331 (centred), with 0--4 of 5 per length in both. The gain is paid for in detection delay. The true-positive rate is 0 for $n=8$--19 and 1/3 at $n=20$--24, and it reaches 2/3 at $n=25$; the single-read-out level reached it at $n=15$ and the two earliest engines from $n{=}8$. The mean true-positive rate over the sweep falls from 0.667 to 0.624 (single read-out) and 0.563 (spent).

Two further observations qualify the zero. First, it is no longer owed to pruning. With the single-read-out level, a non-pruning reference model fed the same streams and read with the same predicate called \code{aperiodic\_1} Periodic at 25 of 93 lengths ($n=29$--31, 45--50 and 52--67), a mean false-positive rate of 0.054: at $n{=}64$ this cell's amplitude is 0.329 and its bound $H\,\Theta=0.056<0.1$, a genuine tail event of this seed, roughly 3.7 null standard deviations. With the spent level the same reference model (dotted in Figure~\ref{fig:e2fpr}) has no false positive at any length, because $\delta_{64}=0.2\cdot8/(64\cdot65)\approx3.8\times10^{-4}$. Second, \code{aperiodic\_0} ends Static. With $\lambda{=}1$ its log-odds is a random walk that reached $\pgrad$, and it was previously masked by a spurious Periodic label (centred engine) that demoted it. E2 does not score the Static class; E5 does, over 20 scenes, and finds this failure common (Table~\ref{tab:e5conf}). The amplitude curves confirm the gate of \eqref{eq:amp}: for the 4-on/4-off door the amplitude is exactly 0 at $n{=}6<T$, 0.653 at every sampled multiple of $T$ ($n=8,16,\dots,64$) and 0.581 at $n{=}12$, where the uncentred v0.1.0 amplitude had overshot to 0.871 (Figure~\ref{fig:e2}). The miss, the 25\%-duty door, is not a failure of the test: the reference model detects it at every length from $n{=}57$ with the spent level (from $n{=}26$ at a single read-out), with amplitude 0.462 at $n{=}64$. The live pipeline never gets there, for a reason that follows from the update rule.

\begin{proposition}[Prune/maturity race]\label{prop:race}
Under the E2 parameters ($\lhit{=}1$, $\lmiss{=}{-}1$, $\lambda{=}1$, $\pprune{=}0.05$, $T{=}8$), a cell touched every window with the 2-on/6-off pattern is erased by \eqref{eq:prune} within at most 7 consecutive touched windows of its creation. Hence $n_i<T$ throughout every lifetime, $a_i=0$, and the cell is never classified Periodic.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
With $\lambda{=}1$ and unit increments, $\ell_i$ is an integer walk from $0$ (the clamp at $\pm5$ never binds below). Since $\sigma(-2)\approx0.119\ge\pprune>\sigma(-3)\approx0.047$, erasure happens exactly when $\ell_i$ first reaches $-3$, provided $a_i<\amin$, which holds while $n_i<T$. If the cell is created with $j\ge3$ vacant windows left in its cycle, it reaches $-3$ after 3 windows. If $j\le2$ vacant windows remain, $\ell_i$ reaches $-j$, then $2-j$ after the two occupied windows, then $-3$ after $5-j$ further vacant windows: $7$ windows in total. If it is created in an occupied window, $\ell_i\le2$ after at most two hits and $-3$ after five more misses, again at most 7. In every case the lifetime is at most $7<T{=}8$ touched windows, so $a_i=0$ by \eqref{eq:amp} and $\pi_i$ is false. Erasure also needs $\lnot g_i$, which holds because $\ell_i\le2$ throughout, so $p_i\le\sigma(2)\approx0.881<\pgrad{=}0.9$ and the cell never graduates. Erasure discards $\boldsymbol{\phi}_i$, so each re-created cell starts again from $n_i=0$.
\end{proof}
The race couples the prune rule \eqref{eq:prune} to the touched-window gate; the reference model, which never prunes, is not subject to it.

\noindent\textbf{Per-dimension cost of the shared engine (E4).} \EfourResult{} The live-cell counts are exact: \code{voxel3d} ends each run holding 10.0$\times$, 6.5$\times$ and 4.4$\times$ as many cells, because its half-voxel ray march creates a hash entry for every traversed free voxel, whereas exact 2D Bresenham clears a bounded strip. We measured neither ray steps nor the per-cell update cost under each backend separately, so we do not attribute the latency difference to one cause. Memory is not measured: the harness multiplies the live-cell count by a fixed 56~B per cell (per-cell evidence and hash node with periodicity off; table and backend overhead excluded), which gives 37.0~MB for the largest 3D configuration (661,410 cells).

\noindent\textbf{What a deployed map does.} E0 and E2 read a cell once per run. The map labels every cell after every window, so the event that matters for a deployed map is that a non-periodic cell is labelled Periodic at \emph{some} window of the run. E5 measures this event directly on held-out seeds, for the live detector with pruning, re-creation and re-testing, over horizons of 64, 256 and 1024 windows (Table~\ref{tab:e5}; every configuration in Table~\ref{tab:e5full}).

\noindent\textbf{The single-read-out level does not control it.} With $\delta{=}0.1$ applied at every read-out, up to 84 of 2000 Bernoulli clutter cells are labelled Periodic within 64 windows and 953 of 2000 within 1024 (E2 parameters, $m{=}0.4$, 95\% interval [0.4547, 0.4984]). The per-window labelling rate stays small, at most 0.0047, but it accumulates over the run. The shipped parameters are affected less, because their slower log-odds walk prunes Bernoulli clutter less often, yet 202 of 2000 cells still flip at some window ($m{=}0.3$).

\noindent\textbf{The spent level does.} With $\delta{=}0.2$ spent over the touch count, chosen on the calibration seeds (Table~\ref{tab:e5choice}), at most 2 of 2000 Bernoulli clutter cells are ever labelled Periodic within 1024 windows in every held-out configuration, a Wilson 95\% upper limit of 0.0036, and the per-window rate is at most $3.9{\times}10^{-6}$. Always-occupied and always-free cells are never labelled Periodic under either rule. The proved bound of Proposition~\ref{prop:spend} covers the never-pruned case at 0.2. For pruned clutter the proved bound $\delta\,\mathbb{E}[L_N]$ is vacuous (a Bernoulli(0.4) cell under the E2 parameters begins about 62 histories), and the measured rate sits far below it: the Chernoff bound is conservative, and successive read-outs of one history are strongly correlated, so the union bound over them is loose.

\noindent\textbf{Correlated clutter is flagged.} The Markov cells violate the independence the bound assumes: their occupancy has real power at low frequencies. Under the shipped $T{=}24$ the spent test labels 1908 of 2000 of them Periodic within 1024 windows (1998 at a single read-out); at $T{=}8$ it labels 2. The periodogram test cannot tell a slow random process from a period of 24 windows; this is a limitation of the null, not a failure of the bound.

\noindent\textbf{What it costs.} On held-out seeds the median window from which a noiseless 4-on/4-off door stays Periodic moves from 15 to 25, a 2-on/2-off door from 13 to 20, a 3-on/5-off door from 21 to 31, and a 4-on/4-off door with 10\% flipped windows from 26 to 48; at the shipped $T{=}24$ a 12-on/12-off door stays at 24 windows, and with 10\% flips it moves from 25 to 40. No door run fails to become Periodic.

\noindent\textbf{Four-class confusion.} Table~\ref{tab:e5conf} scores all four labels over 20 held-out scenes. Under the E2 parameters walls are Static and dynamic cells Transient or Unknown, with one exception in 1000. No cell other than a door is Periodic. All 800 50\%-duty doors are Periodic and none of the 400 25\%-duty doors, which Proposition~\ref{prop:race} proves are never Periodic; the recall of the Periodic class is therefore a property of the scene mix, and we report counts per cell type rather than a recall. Within 256 windows the single-read-out level labels 219 of 2000 aperiodic cells Periodic at some window, the spent level 1. The dominant error is elsewhere: 1221 of 2000 aperiodic Bernoulli(0.5) cells end falsely Static, a share of 0.611 [0.589, 0.632].

\noindent\textbf{False Static labels (E6).} The periodicity test does not decide this error; the graduation rule does, and decay does not remove it. With $\lambda{=}0.97$ added to the E2 parameters, 1042 of 2000 aperiodic cells still end Static. With the shipped defaults 1960 of 2000 do, a share of 0.980 [0.973, 0.985] (1983 after 256 windows), and so do 11 of 1000 dynamic cells; of the 1200 doors of that scene 1091 are Periodic after 64 windows and all 1200 after 256 (Table~\ref{tab:e5conf}). With $\lambda{=}0.97$ all 1200 doors of the E2 scenes are Periodic, the 25\%-duty ones included; we did not analyse why the race of Proposition~\ref{prop:race} is not lost there. Remark~\ref{rem:drift} gives the reason for the Static labels: the shipped $\lhit{=}0.85$ and $\lmiss{=}{-}0.4$ put the mean-field threshold at $m^\dagger=0.354$, so a cell that is occupied in half of its windows drifts up to the clamp. Table~\ref{tab:e6} measures the transition. With the shipped defaults, 396 of 2000 Bernoulli(0.25) cells are Static after 256 windows, 783 at $m{=}0.30$, 1279 at $0.35$, 1698 at $0.40$ and 1974 at $0.50$; and 485 of 2000 Bernoulli(0.10) cells are Static at some window. Symmetric increments ($\lmiss=-\lhit$) move $m^\dagger$ to 0.525 and the count at $m{=}0.50$ to 1156, but they cost noisy walls: of Bernoulli(0.60) cells, which E3 scores as walls, 1741 of 2000 are then Static, against 1998 with the shipped defaults. No parameter set of the sweep separates the two populations. On the 0.05 grid the share of Static cells rises from below 10\% to above 95\% over an interval of $m$ that is 0.30 wide with the shipped defaults and 0.35 wide with the other three sets. A user who knows the occupancy rate of the clutter to reject and of the walls to keep can place $m^\dagger$ between them through $\lmiss$ and $\lambda$, as far as the two rates lie further apart than that interval. We measured only these four sets, on iid cells touched in every window, and changed no default.

\noindent\textbf{Hysteresis band versus decay (E3).} E3 ablates the two stabilisers of the static layer on identical replayed noise (Table~\ref{tab:e3}, full sweep in Table~\ref{tab:e3full}). Removing the hysteresis band has the largest effect in the sweep: at $\pgrad{=}\pdem{=}0.9$ and $\lambda{=}0.90$, the worst configuration, the wall layer flickers 574 times and final F1 drops to 0.810 (recall 0.68), whereas the same noise and decay with the band widened to 0.6 ($\pdem{=}0.3$) flickers 54 times at F1 1.000, a more than tenfold reduction attributable to the band alone. Among zero-band configurations at $\lambda{=}0.90$, flicker rises with the coincident threshold (250 at 0.7, 343 at 0.8, 574 at 0.9), even though a higher threshold is usually the more conservative choice: a threshold pair with no gap is not a substitute for a band wherever it sits. Decay is second order. Holding the worst pair fixed, flicker falls as forgetting slows (574 at $\lambda{=}0.90$, 150 at 0.97, 122 at 1.0), because faster decay lets noisy log-odds cross the coincident threshold more often; with the widest band, decay barely matters (52--54 toggles across all three settings). Hysteresis, not decay, is what keeps the static layer stable (Figure~\ref{fig:e3}).

\noindent\textbf{Periodicity on (E3).} Rerunning the sweep with periodicity on at the shipped defaults leaves final F1 unchanged in every configuration (last column of Table~\ref{tab:e3full}), and flicker is now identical to that with periodicity off in every configuration. The total over the 36 configurations is 3672 in both cases, against 3792 with the single-read-out level, 5472 with the centred amplitude-only rule and 7132 with v0.1.0. The widest-band configuration at $\lambda{=}0.90$ has 54 toggles, against 58 (single read-out), 102 (centred) and 138 (v0.1.0). These walls are free in 40\% of their windows by construction, so their centred amplitude is sampling noise. Under the amplitude-only rule it crossed $\amin$ often, and the unbanded $\lor\,\pi_i$ demotion (\S\ref{subsec:schmitt}) toggled the wall out of the Static layer. With the single-read-out level, 1 or 2 such demotions remained per configuration, because each wall was re-tested in every window. With the spent level, the probability that $\pi_i$ demotes a wall during one uninterrupted history is at most $\delta$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:spend}); none is demoted in any configuration.

The sprt arm repeats E0--E6 on the same scenes and seeds with \code{evidence\_model = sprt} at the shipped pair (Table~\ref{tab:sprtarm}); the harness changed only by taking the arm as an argument, and the legacy arm still reproduces its committed files byte for byte (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}). Nothing below is tuned: the periodic levels are chosen again by the pre-stated rules of E0 and E5 and come out as before, $\delta=\SqEzeroLevel$ for a single read-out and $\delta=\SqEfiveLevel$ spent over the touch count.

\noindent\textbf{E1.} With the sequential test, recall reaches 1.0 at the window index $t=\SqEoneFirst$, the derived \SqEoneFirstWindow{}th window, in all six runs, and the final precision is \SqEonePrecMin{} in every run. The false Static cells that the v0.1 rule created under \SqMoversHigh{} movers per window (precision \SqEoneLegGrid{} for \code{grid2d} and \SqEoneLegVox{} for \code{voxel3d}) do not appear.

\noindent\textbf{E2, E5.} In E2 the Periodic class has \SqEtwoTp/\SqEtwoPos{} true positives and \SqEtwoFp/\SqEtwoNeg{} false positives at $n=64$; the 25\%-duty door that the prune/maturity race cost the legacy arm is Periodic, because the periodicity gate keeps the cell alive until $T$ touched windows exist. In E5 the largest number of Bernoulli clutter cells of 2000 that are ever labelled Periodic within 1024 windows on held-out seeds is \SqEfiveWorst{} (Wilson 95\% upper limit \SqEfiveWorstHi), against \SqEfiveLegWorst{} in the legacy arm: the 0.01 target is met only at the margin. Temporally correlated clutter at the shipped period is labelled Periodic in \SqEfiveMarkov{} of 2000 cells (legacy: \SqEfiveMarkovLeg{}), so the limitation of the null is unchanged.

\noindent\textbf{E3.} E3 has no $\pgrad$, $\pdem$ or $\lambda$ in the sprt arm; it sweeps four pairs at three error rates instead (Table~\ref{tab:e3sprt}). At the shipped pair with $\alpha=\beta=0.01$ the wall layer has recall \SqEthreeRecall{} and \SqEthreeFlicker{} toggles; over the \SqEthreeRows{} rows the flicker lies between \SqEthreeFlickerLo{} and \SqEthreeFlickerHi. The latency and the number of all-miss windows that demote a wall are consequences of the pair and the error rates, not tuned constants.

\noindent\textbf{E6.} With the shipped pair, \SqEsixShipped{} of \SqCellsAperiodic{} Bernoulli(0.5) cells are Static at window 64, against \SqEsixShippedLeg{} in the legacy arm, and \SqEsixDyn{} of \SqCellsDynamic{} dynamic cells; \SqEsixIntermittent{} of the \SqCellsAperiodic{} is read out Intermittent after 256 windows. A Bernoulli(0.5) cell lies between $m_0$ and $m_1$ and above the zero-drift rate $m^\ast=\SqMstar$, where the test promises nothing and drifts upward. After 256 windows, \SqEsixAtTw{} of \SqCellsAperiodic{} Bernoulli(0.20) cells are Static (\SqEsixEverTw{} at some window), \SqEsixAtThr{} at $m=0.30$, \SqEsixAtFour{} at $0.40$, \SqEsixAtHalf{} at $0.50$, \SqEsixAtSix{} at $0.60$ and \SqEsixAtSev{} at $0.70$. Of the \SqDoorsTotal{} doors of the shipped-default scene, \SqEsixDoors{} are Periodic after 64 windows and all \SqEsixDoorsLate{} after 256. The share of Static cells rises with $m$ along the operating characteristic of Table~\ref{tab:e7oc}, not at a point.

\noindent\textbf{E4 and E7.} The cell counts of E4 grow slightly, for example \SqEfourGridLeg{} to \SqEfourGrid{} live cells for \code{grid2d} at $100^2$; no timing was recorded for the sequential test. E7 (Section~\ref{subsec:oc}, Tables~\ref{tab:e7oc} and~\ref{tab:e7ac}) compares the engine with the exact lattice computation and Wald's closed form and measures the effect of autocorrelation; it is the experiment behind the claim that the error rates hold for independent windows.
