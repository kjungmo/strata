\section{Evaluation on Held-Out Real Data}\label{sec:realeval}

This section reports what seven evidence rules do on real LiDAR data that none of them was fitted to. It tunes nothing and picks no winner: the pair of the sequential test was fixed before the first test-set run, and the tables show how the answers move with the rule. The wording of the outcomes is ours and follows the numbers of the tables; the pre-registration (\nolinkurl{PREREG_P3.md}, with dated amendments) fixes the datasets, the arms, the metrics and the reporting rules before the first run. Before it was committed, harness validation had opened a few test-split files: the first scans of Keble~03 and its trajectory, the first frames and the pose files of one Boreas session, the first frames of both KTH sequences, and the first bag messages and the reference files of both LILocBench test sequences (\nolinkurl{PREREG_P3.md}, provenance section, and the access log). Seven amendments were committed before the first evaluation step. The later nine were made during or after the evaluation (\nolinkurl{eval_start.txt} and \nolinkurl{deviations.md} give the order): they handle failed runs and resumption, the zip reader and the cache-frame read, the populations of two Spires rows, the ever-Static bound, the report-only rows, the re-pin of the engine tree, and corrections of the report. None of them changes an arm, a window, a resolution or a threshold of a run. The memory limit of the Spires runs at 0.1~m was raised once, from a lower value to the limit of $9$~GB. Amendments that define or correct a reported row after results existed say so, and the rows are marked.

\subsection{Design}\label{subsec:p3design}

\noindent\textbf{Arms.} All arms run through the same engine, with a window update per touched window, so that the rows differ only in the evidence rule.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{shipped v0.1}: the rule of the first release, $\lhit{=}0.85$, $\lmiss{=}-0.4$, leak 0.97, graduate at $0.8$ after three touched windows, demote at $0.45$ (\code{evidence\_model = legacy}).
  \item \emph{symmetric}: the same with $\lmiss=-\lhit$.
  \item \emph{OctoMap parameters on window evidence}: the probabilities $0.7$ and $0.4$ of the OctoMap sensor model \citep{hornung2013octomap} as increments, its clamps, no leak, occupied if $\ell>0$, never pruned. The increments are applied once per touched window, not once per ray and scan, so the row is not OctoMap and its numbers are not comparable to published OctoMap rows.
  \item \emph{ELite factors on window evidence}: odds factors $9$ and $1/9$ per touched window, no leak, occupied if $\ell>0$. These are the factors that Eq.~(5) of ELite \citep{gil2025elite} gives for an endpoint and for a sampled free point at zero distance from the ray (our arithmetic from the printed equations; the paper states no clamp, and we set $\pm50$). The row is not ELite.
  \item \emph{SPRT $(0.20,0.70)$}: the sequential test of Section~\ref{sec:seq} at the shipped pair, $\alpha=\beta=0.01$; \emph{SPRT low end} $(0.20,0.65)$ and \emph{high end} $(0.30,0.75)$ are the pairs at the ends of the calibration interval (Section~\ref{sec:calib}). These two rows show the dependence on the pair; they change nothing about the first.
\end{itemize}
The OctoMap and ELite arms have no Undecided state: ``not Static'' is ``Rejected'' for them. We did not build an arm for MTD-Map \citep{kim2026mtdmap}: we found no released code, and a reimplementation from the paper alone would fix choices that the paper does not fix.

\noindent\textbf{Datasets and what each can show.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{LILocBench} \citep{trekel2025lilocbench}, \texttt{lt\_changes\_dynamics\_0} (with people) and \texttt{lt\_changes\_0} (a control without people), 2D, 0.05~m cells on a 60~m by 60~m grid with its corner at $(-30,-30)$~m, larger than the grid of the shipped file (pre-registration), 15 scans per window (1.0~s). The truth is a reference map of the \texttt{mapping} session: a cell that was free there and is hit here is clutter or moved furniture, a reference-occupied cell is structure. No per-object truth exists. The test sequences come from the same building and robot as the calibration sequences, so they test the pair on new recordings and new people, not on a new environment.
  \item \emph{Oxford Spires} \citep{tao2024spires}, Keble sequences 03 to 05 against the terrestrial-laser map, 3D, 0.2~m and 0.1~m voxels, 10 and 20 scans per window. The sequences are from one day; there are no seasons and no doors. The clutter class is hits that the terrestrial map does not contain.
  \item \emph{KTH dynamic-map benchmark} \citep{zhang2023dynamic}, KITTI 05 (64 beams) and the semi-indoor sequence (16 beams), 3D, one pass, 0.2~m voxels and, for the semi-indoor sequence only, 0.1~m voxels (KITTI 05 at 0.1~m was not run, by pre-registration, for memory), windows of 10 and 2 frames. Score: static accuracy SA, dynamic accuracy DA, their geometric mean AA, with the benchmark's point-wise rule (the evaluator applies a $0.05$~m tolerance in double precision). A single pass cannot show lifelong behaviour.
  \item \emph{Boreas} \citep{burnett2022boreas}, three sessions of one route in late autumn, spring and summer, 3D, 0.2~m, ranges 3 to 25~m, Periodic veto off (memory; first amendment of the pre-registration). There is no truth for structure or clutter; the sessions are compared with each other.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Settings and reporting rules.} Cells with fewer than five touched windows are not eligible for any rate. The Periodic veto is the same for all arms of a run: on is the primary setting (the node ships it), off the secondary setting, which isolates the evidence rule; the Boreas stage runs with the veto off only. Each run records host, load, commit, parameters and the checksums of its inputs, and every run is reported, the failed ones included: \PtRunsOk{} runs ended with return code zero and \PtRunsBad{} with a non-zero one. A further \PtAttempts{} earlier attempts are kept beside the final records (repeats after a crash, rebuilds and repeated checks). Among them, \PtRepeatOk{} Keble 04 runs failed on a truncated read of the frame cache and passed on a repeat with an identical command after the reader was changed to retry a failed read (eleventh amendment), one Keble 03 run at 0.1~m failed at a lower memory limit and was repeated at the limit of $9$~GB, and the first conversion of Keble 04 failed because the zip reader did not support zip64 archives.

\noindent\textbf{What is in the loop.} Every real-data number comes from the offline harness \code{p3\_probe}, which runs \code{strata\_core} without ROS on the reference poses of each dataset and counts windows in scans. For LILocBench the scans are shifted against the poses by $\PtLilOffset$~s, the value fixed before the evaluation. The ROS node was not in the loop, so TF latency, transport loss, localiser error and stamp offsets enter no real-data number. Intervals are Wilson intervals, which treat cells as independent, and, where tiles are known, the 95\% percentile interval of a bootstrap over 10~m tiles. The block interval is the one to read. Intervals are per row, are not adjusted for the number of rows, and no hypothesis test is run. Two kinds of row were defined after results existed: the near-wall rows of Table~\ref{tab:p3wall} and the ever-Static bound of the Spires clutter rows; they are marked report-only, and the bound is not used in the text. The populations and labels of the Spires latency and end-of-run false-Static rows were fixed by the twelfth amendment, written after the Keble~03 runs and part of the Keble~04 runs at 0.2~m had finished, before any table was written.

\subsection{Nominal against realised false Static labels (LILocBench)}\label{subsec:p3lil}

\input{tables/p3_lil}

The level $\alpha=0.01$ is a bound for the first test of a history under independent windows (Proposition~\ref{prop:ville}); we call it the nominal level per history. The quantity in Table~\ref{tab:p3lil} is per cell: the share of eligible clutter cells that were Static at the close of any window of the run. A rejected cell is erased and its next observation (a hit or a miss) starts a new history, so the unit differs from the bound, and the windows of a cell are autocorrelated (Table~\ref{tab:e7ac}). For SPRT $(0.20,0.70)$ the share is \PtLilAllSprt\,\% of the \PtLilEligible{} eligible clutter cells (Wilson [\PtLilAllLo, \PtLilAllHi]; tile-block interval [\PtLilBlockLo, \PtLilBlockHi]). Restricted to the \PtLilLeN{} clutter cells whose observed hit rate is at most $m_0$ it is \PtLilLeSprt\,\% (Wilson [\PtLilLeLo, \PtLilLeHi]; tile-block interval [\PtLilLeBlockLo, \PtLilLeBlockHi]). \emph{Both are above $\alpha$}, and we do not claim that the realised rate is within the nominal level. On the calibration sequences the same code and pair gave \CalRepLe\,\% at or below $m_0$ (Section~\ref{subsec:calibresult}); the held-out rate is \PtRatioLe{} times this row, on the same eligibility rule. For all clutter the calibration row (\CalRepAll\,\%) also counts cells touched fewer than five times, which the held-out rows exclude, so the ratio of \PtRatioAll{} compares different populations. Table~\ref{tab:e7ac} shows the direction of the effect: autocorrelation of the windows raises the per-forward-test share above $\alpha$ (ratio \SqAcTw{} for the engine at lag-1 autocorrelation $0.23$, \SqRefWalk{} for the bare walk). Restarts alone also exceed $\alpha$ with independent windows: \SqEsixEverTw{} of \SqCellsAperiodic{} Bernoulli($m_0$) cells of E6 are Static at some window within 256 windows, a per-cell share that the per-history level does not bound. The four arms other than the three SPRT arms graduate more clutter. Over all eligible clutter cells, shipped v0.1 gives \PtLilAllShipped\,\%, symmetric \PtLilAllSymm\,\%, OctoMap parameters on window evidence \PtLilAllOcto\,\% and ELite factors on window evidence \PtLilAllElite\,\%; at or below $m_0$ they give \PtLilLeShipped\,\%, \PtLilLeSymm\,\%, \PtLilLeOcto\,\% and \PtLilLeElite\,\%. The two ends of the pair give \PtLilAllLow\,\% and \PtLilAllHigh\,\% for all clutter.

The control without people, \texttt{lt\_changes\_0}, gives \PtCtlAllSprt\,\% over all clutter and \PtCtlLeSprt\,\% at or below $m_0$ for SPRT $(0.20,0.70)$. The all-clutter rate is close to the rate with people. Moved furniture legitimately puts structure into cells that were free in the mapping session, so the rate of \texttt{lt\_changes\_dynamics\_0} is an upper bound for people alone, and the control does not isolate the effect of people either. A person who lingers in a cell can produce a hit rate above $m_0$, and a hit-rate test cannot tell such a person from a wall.

\noindent\textbf{Undecided cells.} In the arms with an Undecided state, most clutter cells end the run Undecided: \PtLilUndSprt\,\% for SPRT $(0.20,0.70)$ and \PtLilUndShipped\,\% for shipped v0.1. The two binary arms end \PtLilRejOcto\,\% (OctoMap parameters) and \PtLilRejElite\,\% (ELite factors) of the clutter cells Rejected; SPRT $(0.20,0.70)$ rejects \PtLilRejSprt\,\%. Rejection waits for the touched windows of the periodicity gate, which is one reason for the Undecided share of the sequential arms; we did not measure how much of it the gate explains.

\subsection{What these runs cannot show}\label{subsec:p3cannot}

The runs cannot show per-object truth (LILocBench has none), seasons and doors (Spires covers one day), a removal-benchmark score (the KTH sequences are a single pass, the pair is not calibrated for 3D, and the OctoMap and ELite rows are not the published methods), or any false-Static or false-reject truth in Boreas. They cannot show that Periodic is right on real data, because no sequence has a door with a logged schedule. They show how seven rules differ on the same cells and how a stated nominal level relates to what happened on real clutter.
