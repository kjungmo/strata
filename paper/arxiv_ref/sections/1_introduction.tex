\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

An occupancy grid built the textbook way accumulates evidence and never lets go of it \citep{thrun2005probabilistic}. For a robot that drives a route once this is harmless; for one that maps the same warehouse or hospital corridor for weeks it is a slow failure. A cart parked for an afternoon, a person walking past and a load-bearing wall all leave the same kind of mark, and after enough passes the map describes the union of everything the sensor ever touched rather than the building. Lifelong mapping has to hold three requirements at once: the map must be \emph{durable} enough to keep structure that is genuinely permanent, \emph{plastic} enough to forget structure that has moved on, and it must do both without one global forgetting rate that is simultaneously too slow to erase movers and too fast to trust a wall---the stability--plasticity dilemma that \citet{biber2005dynamicmaps} framed for dynamic maps. A third requirement complicates the split: some structure is \emph{cyclic}, such as a door open every morning and shut every night. Its occupancy is a periodic temporal signal, and a flat decay law erases exactly the regularity that makes it predictable \citep{krajnik2017fremen,krajnik2014spectral}.

Per-cell models of these requirements are not new. Temporal occupancy grids \citep{arbuckle2002temporal} classify cells by their occupancy over several timescales, and \citet{mitsou2007temporal} keep each cell's occupancy history to separate static, low-dynamic and high-dynamic cells. The closest prior work is the FreMEn line: \citet{krajnik2016persistent} maintain a 2D spatio-temporal occupancy grid that represents the persistence and periodicity of individual cells and was evaluated on several days of patrols in an open-plan office, and FROctomap \citep{krajnik2014froctomap} attaches the same spectral model to the voxels of an octree. Recent LiDAR systems place the persistence decision inside larger pipelines. ELite \citep{gil2025elite} updates a per-point ephemerality with a Bayes rule; LT-mapper \citep{kim2022ltmapper} and Khronos \citep{schmid2024khronos} resolve durability against plasticity as stages of full lifelong-SLAM or metric-semantic systems; and MTD-Map \citep{kim2026mtdmap}, concurrent with this work, maintains static, dynamic and transition maps from per-voxel occupancy statistics. Dynamic-point removal methods decide the same question offline or per scan \citep{kim2020removert,lim2021erasor,duberg2024dufomap,jia2024beautymap}.

Several of these systems keep a number per cell or point that hits push up and misses push down, and compare it with a threshold. As far as we can tell from the papers, none of them states the test that the number performs: which two hit rates it tells apart, and with which error. For the log-odds accumulator of OctoMap \citep{hornung2013octomap} the answer is a short calculation, and it is not what a user would choose. The increments $(+0.85,-0.4)$ are the increments of Wald's sequential probability ratio test \citep{wald1947sequential} between the hit rates \SqLegMzero{} and \SqLegMone{} (Section~\ref{sec:seq}). A cell whose hit rate is above about a third then drifts toward Static, and a leak that shrinks the statistic every window removes the property on which a bound on the error rests. The first release of \sname\ used exactly this rule, and in a synthetic test \SqEsixShippedLeg{} of \SqCellsAperiodic{} Bernoulli(0.5) clutter cells ended Static.

We present \emph{\sname} 0.2, a SLAM-free persistence-and-periodicity engine in which the test is explicit (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}). The user states the hit rate $m_0$ at or below which a cell is clutter, the hit rate $m_1$ at or above which it is structure, and two error rates; increments and thresholds are derived, and every cell ends Static, Rejected or Undecided. The same per-cell state machine serves a fixed-array 2D grid and a sparse 3D voxel hash, which differ only in the point-to-key map and the ray walk: the state machine never sees coordinates, the engine (\code{strata\_core}) links no ROS, DDS, TF or PCL, and choosing 2D or 3D is one runtime string parameter. We state the scope up front: \sname\ is not SLAM (it consumes an external \code{map}$\to$sensor transform), it models single-session persistence rather than multi-session remapping, its periodicity test checks one configured base period and its harmonics rather than selecting periods as FreMEn does, and its default pair is provisional. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{The test, made explicit.} The increments of a log-odds accumulator determine a pair of hit rates (Proposition~\ref{prop:invert}). The OctoMap increments, which the first release used, are a sequential probability ratio test between \SqLegMzero{} and \SqLegMone{} (Section~\ref{subsec:seqv01}), and a multiplicative leak breaks the supermartingale property that an error bound needs (Proposition~\ref{prop:leak}). We did not find an earlier statement of either fact. ELite \citep{gil2025elite} already accumulates a likelihood ratio per point, symmetric at zero distance from the ray, and MTD-Map \citep{kim2026mtdmap} already maintains a per-voxel Markov statistic; we cite both, run the factors of ELite as one arm of the evaluation, and did not build an arm for MTD-Map because we found no released code.
  \item \emph{Defaults derived from stated error rates.} From $(m_0,m_1,\alpha,\beta)$ the engine derives increments, thresholds, a cap and a CUSUM-like reverse test for demotion, with three outcomes instead of two. The periodicity gate is the parameter $T$ (\code{period\_windows}), a fixed default not derived from the pair. The truncation $n_{\max}$ was computed once, offline, for the shipped pair by a pre-registered rule; the engine does not recompute it when the pair changes. Under independent windows the probability that clutter at a rate of at most $m_0$ graduates in the first test of one cell history (up to its first graduation, its first rejection or its retention as a periodic candidate) is at most $\alpha/(1-\beta)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:ville}, by Ville's inequality); restarts after a demotion are not covered. The operating characteristic measured on the engine with the Periodic veto off matches the exact lattice computation and is approximated by Wald's closed form (E7), and the committed Monte Carlo shows how autocorrelated windows inflate the rate.
  \item \emph{Calibration and held-out evaluation on real data, reported as measured.} We calibrated the pair on LILocBench under a pre-registered rule with quality gates, amended after the first calibration measurement; the gate failed, nothing froze, and the shipped pair is provisional (Section~\ref{sec:calib}). We then ran seven evidence rules on LILocBench, Oxford Spires, the KTH benchmark and Boreas without tuning and without choosing a winner (Section~\ref{sec:realeval}). The realised per-cell false-Static rates are above the nominal per-history level, and we say so.
  \item \emph{One geometry-free engine for two geometries, with a periodic label that has a proved false-alarm bound for iid clutter.} A single \code{int64}-keyed engine (\code{LayeredMap} with \code{PeriodicityModel}) drives both backends through one \code{MapBackend} interface and builds with C++17 and Eigen alone under 114 behaviour-level tests. A known moment-generating-function bound \citep{hsu2012tail}, applied to the floating-mean periodogram \citep{zechmeister2009gls} and spent over the touch count in the manner of alpha spending \citep{lan1983discrete}, bounds the probability that the periodic label fires on iid clutter. The periodic label is validated on synthetic streams only.
\end{itemize}

In the synthetic suite, which runs in two arms (the first release's arithmetic and the sequential test), the sequential test graduates an always-hit wall at its derived \SqEoneFirstWindow{}th window instead of the third and labels \SqEsixShipped{} instead of \SqEsixShippedLeg{} of the \SqCellsAperiodic{} Bernoulli(0.5) clutter cells Static, a rate that the pair does not promise to control between its two hypotheses. On real data, the sequential test graduates fewer clutter cells than the first release's rule, waits longer to graduate walls and leaves more structure Undecided; none of the compared rules is shown to be a good removal method on these data (Section~\ref{sec:realeval}). Whether the periodic test, whose false-alarm bound holds for iid clutter, improves on FreMEn's own component selection, and whether it finds doors in real data, remain to be evaluated (Section~\ref{sec:disc}).
